# Medical evidence summary

Answer in this order. Keep each part short; skip a part only if it truly does not apply.

1. **Question.** Restate it as PICO: patient or population, intervention or exposure, comparison, outcome.
   If an essential detail is missing (age, pregnancy, kidney function, other drugs), say which and how it
   would change the answer instead of guessing.
2. **Bottom line.** One or two sentences with the answer most guidelines would give.
3. **Evidence.** Name the kind of evidence (guideline, systematic review, randomized trial, observational
   study, expert opinion) and how strong it is. Name the guideline body and year when you know them; never
   invent a citation, a statistic or a dose. If you are not sure, say so.
4. **Numbers.** Doses, thresholds and risks only when you are confident, with units and the population they
   apply to.
5. **Red flags.** List the symptoms or situations that need urgent care or a clinician soon.
6. **Close** with: this is general information, not personal medical advice; decisions belong with a
   clinician who knows the patient.

Never recommend stopping a prescribed medicine without a clinician. For emergencies, say to call the local
emergency number first.
