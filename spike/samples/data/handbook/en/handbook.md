# Olive House Library: Handbook for Members and Volunteers

*Invented sample document. The Olive House Library, its people and its rules do not exist.*

## 1. About the library

The Olive House Library is a small neighbourhood library that opened in a restored house with a courtyard and an old
olive tree. It is run by a team of three paid librarians and about forty volunteers, and it is funded by the district
council, by modest membership donations and by the yearly book sale. The collection holds around eighteen thousand
items: novels, non-fiction, comics, children's books, local history, films on disc, audio books and a small shelf of
board games that members can borrow like any other item.

Our mission is simple. We want every person in the neighbourhood, whatever their age or income, to find something to
read, a quiet place to work and a friendly face when they need help. We do not sell anything inside the building and
we do not show advertising. We keep as little data about you as we can, and we explain below exactly what we keep.

This handbook answers the questions people ask most often. It is written for members, but volunteers should also
read it, because it describes how we expect everyone to treat each other and the collection. If you cannot find an
answer here, ask at the front desk; the librarians would rather answer a question twice than see a problem grow.

## 2. Opening hours

The library is open Monday to Saturday. From Monday to Friday we open at 9:30 in the morning and close at 7:00 in the
evening, except on Wednesday, when we close at 2:00 in the afternoon so that the team can hold its weekly meeting and
prepare the collection. On Saturday we open at 10:00 and close at 2:00. We are closed on Sundays and on public
holidays.

During the school holidays in summer, from the middle of July until the end of August, the library opens only in the
morning, from 9:30 to 2:00, Monday to Friday, and stays closed on Saturday. The courtyard is open until sunset in the
warm months and members may read there, but the book return box is the only service available outside opening hours.

Special dates are announced on the notice board by the door, on the printed monthly leaflet and on the screen at the
front desk. If the library has to close without warning, for a burst pipe or a power cut, we put a note on the door
and the loans that fall due that day are automatically extended by two days, so nobody is fined for our closure.

## 3. Membership and cards

Anyone who lives, works or studies in the district can join the library for free. To join, bring an identity document
and a recent piece of mail or a student card that shows your address. A librarian will register you in a few minutes
and give you a card with a number. Children under fourteen need an adult to sign for them once; the adult does not
need to be a member.

Visitors who live elsewhere can use the reading rooms and the courtyard without a card. They can also join as
occasional members for three months, paying a small fee of five euros, which allows them to borrow up to three
items at a time.

The card is personal. Please do not lend it to somebody else; if a card is used by another person, the account holder
remains responsible for the items. If you lose your card, tell us as soon as possible. We block the old number the
same day and issue a new card for a fee of two euros. If you change address or telephone number, tell the desk so
that we can still reach you when a reserved item arrives.

Membership is renewed every two years. The librarian will ask you to confirm your details, and nothing else. We do not
ask about your reading, your income or your family. Members who do not use their card for three years are archived,
and their personal details are deleted after another year.

## 4. Borrowing rules

Each adult member can borrow up to ten items at a time. Children can borrow up to six. The loan period depends on
the kind of item. Books, comics and audio books are lent for 21 days. Magazines are lent for 14 days, except for the
latest issue, which stays in the reading room. Films on disc and board games are lent for 7 days, because many people
wait for them.

Reference works, such as dictionaries, atlases and the local archive folders, never leave the building, but you can
read them at the tables and ask us to photocopy a few pages. Photocopies cost ten cents per page and the machine
accepts coins only.

To borrow, bring the items and your card to the self-service machine or to the desk. The machine prints a receipt with
the return date. We recommend keeping it, or taking a photograph of it, because the date is not always the one you
expect when a holiday falls inside the loan period. Items borrowed on a Saturday are due on a Saturday, three weeks
later, or on the next open day if the library is closed.

Some items are marked with a yellow sticker. These are in high demand, such as new releases and the books chosen for
the reading circle. They can be borrowed for 14 days only and cannot be renewed. This rule keeps the waiting list
short and fair.

## 5. Renewals and reservations

If you have not finished a book, you can renew it up to two times, for another 14 days each time, as long as nobody
else has reserved it. You can renew in person, by calling the desk, by writing to the library address or at the
self-service machine. Renewals are possible until the due date; once an item is late it must be brought back first.

To reserve an item that is out on loan, ask at the desk or use the form on the website. You may have up to four
reservations at once. When the item comes back we keep it on the reservation shelf for seven days and we send you a
message by telephone or by email, depending on what you chose when you joined. If you do not collect it within seven
days, it goes to the next person on the list or back to the shelf.

We also accept suggestions. If the library does not have a title you would like to read, fill in a suggestion slip.
The team reviews the slips every Wednesday afternoon and buys what fits the budget and the collection. You will always
get an answer, even if the answer is no, and we will tell you if another library nearby can lend it to you.

Interlibrary loans take between five and ten working days and are free for members. An item that arrives from another
library follows that library's rules, not ours, and the due date is printed on the strip around it.

## 6. Late items and fines

We prefer to have our books back than to collect fines, so our system is gentle. The first day after the due date
brings no consequences. From the second day, an adult's late item costs 15 cents per day, up to a maximum of six euros
per item. Children's items never generate fines; instead we send a friendly reminder to the family.

Films on disc and board games, which have a short loan period, cost 30 cents per day, up to a maximum of six euros as
well. A member whose fines exceed ten euros cannot borrow again until the debt is paid, but can still read in the
building. You can pay at the desk in cash or by card; we always give a receipt.

If you are going to be late, tell us. A librarian can extend a loan by hand in special cases, such as an illness, a
trip, a hospital stay or a flood at home. We never ask for a certificate; your word is enough. Fines are cancelled
when the library makes an error, for example if the machine printed the wrong date.

Three reminders are sent for each late item: a message on the third day, another on the tenth day and a letter on
the thirtieth day. After sixty days the item is considered lost and the rules of the next section apply.

## 7. Lost or damaged items

Accidents happen. If you lose an item or it is damaged beyond use, please tell us instead of hiding it. For a lost
book we ask for the replacement price printed in the catalogue, with a minimum of four euros and a maximum of
thirty. If you prefer, you can bring a new copy of the same edition, or a second-hand copy in good condition, and we
will not charge anything.

A book with a coffee stain, a torn page or a loose cover is not a disaster. Please do not repair it yourself with
sticky tape, because the glue damages the paper over the years. Hand it to the desk and our volunteer binder will
mend it with the right materials, without any charge.

Films on disc and audio books are checked when they come back, and a scratched disc is not charged if the scratch is
small and the disc still plays. If a disc stops working, we ask for the replacement price. Board games are counted
piece by piece; a missing piece is charged at one euro, and the game is replaced if more than three pieces are lost.

If an item has been exposed to bedbugs, mould or a contagious illness at home, please do not bring it in with the
others. Call us first. We will give you a bag and instructions, and nobody will be blamed.

## 8. The children's corner

The children's corner is on the ground floor, next to the courtyard door, and is designed for children from birth
to twelve. It has low shelves, cushions, a small puppet theatre and a table for drawing. Adults who accompany children
are asked to stay with them, because the staff cannot supervise them while working.

Every Tuesday and Thursday at 5:00 in the afternoon there is a story hour. A volunteer reads one or two stories, and
the children can then borrow a book with the help of the librarian. On the first Saturday of each month we hold a
longer session with songs and crafts for families with children under five.

The corner also has a shelf of books in simple language for children who are learning to read, arranged by colour
instead of by number. A green sticker means the first steps, a blue one means short chapters and a red one means
longer stories. Teachers can request a box of twenty books for their classroom, for up to six weeks, by asking at the
desk at least one week ahead.

For the safety of the youngest, the stairs to the first floor have a gate that must stay closed, and the courtyard
door is locked when no adult is present. Please tell us if you see a child alone for a long time.

## 9. Study rooms and computers

The first floor has two study rooms. The silent room has twelve seats, and the group room has a large table for six
people, a whiteboard and permission to talk quietly. Both are free and can be reserved at the desk for up to two
hours at a time, with no more than one reservation per day. Unclaimed reservations are released after fifteen minutes.

There are six computers in the main room, available to members on a first-come, first-served basis, in sessions of
forty-five minutes that can be extended if nobody is waiting. The computers can browse the internet, edit documents
and print. Printing costs ten cents per page in black and white and thirty cents in colour. We do not record the pages
you visit, and the session is wiped when you log out.

The library offers free wireless access throughout the building and the courtyard. The password changes every month and
is printed on the leaflet. Please use headphones for any sound, keep your phone on silent and take calls outside.

Food is not allowed in the study rooms. Water in a closed bottle is welcome. In the courtyard, you may eat a snack,
provided that you take away your rubbish and do not feed the birds that nest in the olive tree.

## 10. Workshops and events

The library holds around fifteen activities a month, all of them free. They are chosen by the team, together with the
members, in an open meeting that takes place each June. The regular activities are the following.

The reading circle meets on the second Thursday of the month at 6:00 in the evening. Each month the members vote for
a book from a short list; there are always twelve copies available to borrow. Newcomers are welcome at any time, and
nobody is expected to have finished the book.

The conversation table for people who are learning the local language meets every Monday at 4:30. It is led by
volunteers and has no exam, no grades and no teacher. The repair afternoon, on the last Saturday of the month, invites
neighbours to bring a broken lamp, a loose zip or a torn jacket and fix it together with volunteers who know how.

The seed library lets you borrow seeds in spring and return a few from your harvest in autumn. The digital help desk,
every Friday morning, helps older members with their phones, forms and online appointments. Finally, the year has two
large events: the book sale in the first weekend of October and the night of stories in the courtyard in June.

To join a workshop that has limited places, register at the desk. If you cannot come, tell us at least a day ahead, so
that another person can use your place.

## 11. Accessibility

The ground floor can be reached without stairs through the side entrance, which has a ramp and an automatic door.
There is a lift to the first floor, a toilet adapted for wheelchairs and a table that can be raised to different
heights. Dogs that assist people are always welcome, and other dogs are welcome in the courtyard.

We keep a selection of books in large print, audio books, books with simple language and picture books without text.
On request, a librarian will read a short document aloud, help fill in a form or accompany a person to a shelf. If you
cannot come, we deliver up to four items every second week to members who live nearby and have difficulty leaving
home; a volunteer brings them and takes them back.

Signs in the library use large letters and the same symbols everywhere. The screen at the front desk can show
the text in a higher contrast. If something in the building makes it difficult for you to come in, to move or to
understand, tell us. We have fixed several problems after a member mentioned them, and we want to continue.

The quiet hour, from 9:30 to 10:30 on Tuesday and Saturday mornings, is intended for people who find noise and
crowds tiring: the lights are lowered, there are no announcements and the machines are kept silent.

## 12. Volunteers

About forty volunteers help the library. They cover the story hour, the book repair, the deliveries to homes, the
shelving and the events. Nobody is expected to give more than two or three hours a week. To volunteer, you must be
sixteen or older and attend a short welcome session, held on the first Monday of each month.

Volunteers receive a badge, a key to the staff entrance only if their task requires it, and a copy of this handbook.
They do not handle money and do not have access to members' personal data; any question about a member's account must
be passed to a librarian. A volunteer who works with children also completes a short course on child protection and
signs a code of conduct.

The team holds a meeting with the volunteers every three months to listen to ideas and complaints, and a thank-you
dinner in December. Volunteers can borrow up to fifteen items, with the same rules as other members. A volunteer who
wants to stop can do so at any time, with no explanation needed; we just ask you to return the badge and tell the
person who coordinates your task, so we can find a replacement.

## 13. Donations of books

We are grateful for donations, but our shelves are not infinite. Please bring no more than two bags at a time, and
tell the desk before you come if you have a large collection. We accept books in good condition, published in the last
twenty years, and local history of any date. We cannot accept encyclopaedias, textbooks, damaged books, books that
smell of damp, magazines older than two years or out-of-date guides.

Each donation is reviewed by a volunteer. If a book is already in the collection and in good condition, it goes to the
book sale. Books that we cannot use are offered to schools, a nursing home and a community fridge that has a small
bookshelf; what is left is recycled as paper.

If you want a receipt for your donation, ask for one and we will give you a list. We cannot give a value to the books.
If you prefer to give money instead, you can leave a donation in the box by the door or ask for the form to become a
friend of the library, with a monthly amount you choose. All donations are recorded in the annual report that is
posted on the notice board each March.

## 14. Privacy and conduct

We keep your name, address, telephone number, card number and the list of items you currently have on loan. Once
you return an item, the link between you and the item is deleted at the end of the same day, so we cannot tell
anyone, including the authorities, what you have read in the past. We do not sell, share or publish your data, and we
do not use cameras inside the reading rooms. There is one camera at the entrance for safety, which keeps images for
seven days.

Everyone in the library has the right to feel safe and respected. We ask you to speak kindly, to keep your voice low,
to leave the shelves tidy and to look after the furniture. We do not tolerate insults, harassment or discrimination of
any kind. Photography is allowed for personal use, but please do not photograph other people without asking them.

If there is a problem with another person, tell a librarian, who will talk to everyone calmly. A person who breaks the
rules is first asked to stop, then asked to leave for the day. Only in serious or repeated cases can a membership be
suspended, and the person always has the right to explain and to ask the district council to review the decision.

Smoking is not allowed anywhere in the building, including the courtyard. Bicycles may be parked in the courtyard
rack, and prams can stay in the entrance hall.

## 15. Emergencies

In case of fire, a medical emergency or any danger, follow the instructions of the staff and the volunteers, who wear
a yellow vest on these occasions. The emergency exits are marked in green and lead to the courtyard and to the side
street. The meeting point is the olive tree. Please do not use the lift in a fire.

The front desk has a first-aid kit and a defibrillator. Three of our librarians and six volunteers have taken a
first-aid course and renew it every two years. The telephone number for emergencies is displayed beside the desk and
on the back of your library card.

If a storm or a power cut forces us to close, the notice board and the website tell you when we will reopen. The
deposit boxes remain available. Any loan that falls due during an unplanned closure is automatically extended, as
explained in the section about opening hours.

## 16. Frequently asked questions

**How many items can I borrow?** Ten if you are an adult and six if you are a child. Occasional members can borrow
three.

**For how long?** Books, comics and audio books: 21 days. Magazines: 14 days. Films and board games: 7 days. Items
with a yellow sticker: 14 days with no renewal.

**Can I renew a book?** Yes, twice, for 14 days each time, unless someone has reserved it or it is already late.

**How much is a fine?** Fifteen cents a day for books, thirty cents a day for films and games, with a maximum of six
euros per item. Children are never fined.

**What if I lose my card?** Tell us the same day. A new card costs two euros.

**Can I use the study rooms without being a member?** Yes, the reading rooms are open to everyone; the study rooms must
be reserved at the desk, and reservation requires a card.

**Where can I return a book when the library is closed?** In the return box by the front door, which is emptied every
morning. Items left there count as returned on the day we open.

**Who can I talk to about a suggestion or a complaint?** Any librarian, or the suggestion box beside the desk. The
team answers every written message in ten working days.
